About CEPAR

A coalition, not a center

CEPAR@Tufts is a group of researchers who chose to organize, to make participatory work easier to do, and to make Tufts a partner communities can trust.

Overview

CEPAR@Tufts is a coalition of Tufts researchers that advocates for inclusive, participatory and collaborative community research. It exists to advance equity, inclusion and justice in how research gets done, while producing scholarship that is rigorous and useful.

It sits within the Tisch College Community Research Center, which convenes it, but the coalition itself is made of researchers across the university rather than staff of one office.

History

Since 2021, a working group of Tufts researchers and community partners has met regularly to learn about each other's work, led by the Tisch College Community Research Center. Out of it came two internal papers of recommendations for promoting this work at Tufts, one of them still in development, and a set of recommendations for the SBER IRB, the point at which participatory research most often stalls.

The coalition now organizes its work through four standing subcommittees.

IRB & ethics

Making review workable for research where the community is a partner rather than a subject.

Funding & finance

Finding and unblocking money, including the mechanics of paying community partners.

Community partnerships

Building and sustaining relationships beyond the length of any single grant.

Campus scholar support

Training, mentorship and recognition for faculty and students doing this work.

Our current goals

  • 01Reduce the bureaucratic obstacles participatory researchers face, and expand university support for the work
  • 02Increase training and opportunities across faculty and students
  • 03Build more ethical and collaborative relationships between Tufts researchers and vulnerable communities

Support

Funding and administrative support have come from the Tisch College Community Research Center, Tufts Springboard, Tisch College of Civic Life, the Eliot-Pearson Department of Child Study and Human Development, and the Department of Sociology. That spread is itself part of the point: the work is not housed in one place.

The coalition also credits the backing of Ayanna Thomas, Augusta Rohrbach, Dayna Cunningham and Taína McField.

About this site

This hub is a first version, built to be useful on day one and maintainable with the smallest possible amount of ongoing work. Some things are left out on purpose, not walled off, just not yet.

  • Natural-language discovery. Describe a problem in ordinary words and get pointed at relevant people, projects and methods.
  • A tool and instrument exchange. Searchable surveys, frameworks and instruments built by this community, with validation notes.
  • Project matching and opportunity postings. Worth doing once the directory is trusted, and premature before that.
  • Richer impact storytelling. Video and other media for community partners, leaders and funders.
