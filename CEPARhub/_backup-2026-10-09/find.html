Directory

Nobody looks for a collaborator alphabetically

So this is organized the way people actually search: by what the work is about, how it is done, who it is with, where, and in which discipline. Combine as many filters as you like.

Today this matches keywords across every field on a record, including what people say they want to work on next, which is often where a collaboration actually starts. The structure is built so a natural-language version can replace it later without re-tagging anyone.

Staying current

Why every profile shows a date

A directory nobody maintains is worse than none, so the upkeep model is visible rather than buried: each record says where it came from and when the person last confirmed it.

Mostly automatic

Pulled from what exists

Name, title, school, department and publications come from Tufts systems already maintained for other reasons. Nobody retypes them, and they never go stale on their own.

One click, twice a year

Confirmed by the person

Topics, methods, communities, current projects, future interests and what someone is open to are theirs alone. Twice a year one email asks: still right? Yes keeps the date fresh. No opens a short form.

A few hours a month

Reviewed by TCRC

Someone checks records flagged needs review, adds new members, and confirms anything naming a community partner. Partner names never appear without the partner's sign-off.

The estimate we intend to test, not assume

Estimated upkeep effort, by activity
Twice-yearly confirmation email (per member, per year)~4 min
TCRC review of flagged records~2 hr / month
Adding a new member or project~10 min each
Estimated ongoing load to keep this useful and current~3 hr / month

A hypothesis, not a finding. Whether people will actually confirm a profile, and which upkeep model they would support, is one of the research questions behind this prototype.

Not finding the right person?

TCRC knows this network better than any directory can. Tell them what you are trying to do and they will point you at someone.
