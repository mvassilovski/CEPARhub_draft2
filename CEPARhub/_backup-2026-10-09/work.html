Projects & Impact

Longer than most people realize

Community-engaged research at Tufts did not start with CEPAR. Here is the longer history, then the work currently running.

History

Three decades, five schools.

Sample entries, a real version would be built from project and publication records that already exist.

1994–2002

Neighborhood health surveys in Somerville, the first Tufts studies designed with a resident advisory board rather than merely reviewed by one.

2003–2011

Food systems partnerships across Chinatown and Medford, and the first shared data-use agreements with partner organizations.

2012–2019

Participatory evaluation becomes standard in several school-based programs; community partners begin co-authoring publications.

2020–2021

Pandemic-era rapid research conducted remotely with partner-led data collection, later published as a methods contribution.

2021–now

The CEPAR working group begins meeting, researchers and community partners convening regularly under TCRC leadership, and grows into a coalition with subcommittees on IRB and ethics, funding, community partnerships and scholar support.

Current work

Six projects running now.

Each one designed with the people it affects, not merely reviewed by them.

Active · 2024–present

Resident-run water sampling network

Residents collect and interpret their own watershed samples. Tufts supplies calibration, analysis and a shared dashboard.

Partner: Mystic River Watershed Association
Tufts lead: Menéndez-Kroll · Engineering

Active · year three

Corner-store fresh produce pilot

Small stores stock and price fresh produce on terms set by a neighborhood advisory board, with health outcomes tracked over time.

Partner: Roxbury Food Coalition
Tufts lead: Oyelaran · Friedman

Active · 2025–present

Doula-led study of birth experience

Birth workers designed the interview protocol and hold decision rights over what is published about their clients.

Partner: Neighborhood Birth Workers Alliance
Tufts lead: Nnadi-Holt · Arts & Sciences

Active · 2023–present

Youth-led neighborhood safety study

Young people are the researchers: they set the questions, conduct the interviews, and present findings to the city themselves.

Partner: Chelsea Youth Collaborative
Tufts lead: Oyelowo-Finn · Arts & Sciences

Completed · 2022–2025

Universal school meals, what changed

A participatory evaluation with families and cafeteria staff. The results informed a district-wide policy decision in 2025.

Partner: Medford Public Schools
Tufts lead: Lindqvist · Friedman

Active · 2026–present

Who gets to see municipal data

City staff and community data groups jointly design disclosure rules for neighborhood-level administrative data.

Partner: City of Somerville, Office of Data
Tufts lead: Duraisamy · Engineering
